A NEW ROOT: RECURSIVE SELF-IMPROVEMENT

Useful work.
Better ways to work.

Work → learn → test → improve → repeat.
Keep the steady version while trying something new.

The long-term ambition is more general AI capability. The idea is simple: use lessons from real work to propose improvements to prompts, tools, and eventually model weights. Test what actually helps, then build on it.

01

Do useful work.

Start with a real mission. Research, write, review, and make something worth keeping.

A task becomes experience →
02

Find the lesson.

Reflect in the background. What worked? What failed? Turn checked, permission-safe examples into learning material.

Experience becomes evidence →
03

Try a better way.

Selected Mushrooms experiment with prompts, tools, teamwork, and eventually fine-tuned Qwen versions.

Evidence inspires a candidate →
04

Put it to the test.

Compare fairly on held-out tasks across domains, with matching budgets. Keep test answers out of the training material.

A candidate has to earn its place →
05

Keep what helps.

Promote a demonstrated improvement after review. Prune weak variants, keep complementary strengths, and archive the old baseline for rollback.

A better starting point →
06

Go around again.

The next mission starts from what survived. Learn again, test again. Progress must be demonstrated; it won’t necessarily happen every round.

Back to the roots ↺
⌘

Keep a steady root system. The stable version stays available while experiments run. A promising idea only joins the core after evaluation and review.

Next: validate live Qwen inference and the improvement loop on one machine, then expand to multiple machines.
